# Research notes — JaeYoun Park portfolio

Last reviewed: 2026-09-22

This file records the public-source provenance used by the portfolio. It is intentionally conservative about individual project credit.

## Verified public profile facts

- Public LinkedIn profile: JaeYoun Park — https://www.linkedin.com/in/jaeyoun-park-37b2a944/
- Public search results identify her with SodiumPartners in the Geneva area and list Schule für Gestaltung Basel (2014).
- The public profile snapshot available to search does **not** expose enough detail to verify a precise role title, complete employment dates, or project-by-project responsibilities. Those details are therefore not invented in the site.

## Asiana Airlines — primary studio source

SodiumPartners official project page:
https://www.sodiumpartners.com/wk-asiana

Documented studio scope:
- Class Naming
- Brand Identity
- Technical Drawing

Documented project description:
- Airline CI developed within the Kumho Asiana Group CI framework.
- Existing Asiana “saekdong wings” / warm-grey heritage reinterpreted as the “Energy of Saekdong” concept.
- Applications documented across aircraft, airport and agency signage, china ware, uniforms, seat design, ticket jackets, shopping bags, website templates and vehicles.
- Economy Class renamed to Travel Class, with class-level BI / representative colors.
- Joseph McGuire Associates, San Francisco is credited by SodiumPartners for aircraft-livery design and technical consulting.

## Important credit boundary: group Wing mark vs airline identity

Contemporary Dong-A Ilbo coverage dated 2006-02-20 says the Kumho Asiana Group’s new red “Wing” CI was first proposed by US CI firm Landor:
https://www.donga.com/news/Economy/article/all/20060220/8276939/1

This is separate from SodiumPartners’ official Asiana Airlines project credit. The site therefore does **not** say that SodiumPartners or JaeYoun Park personally designed the Kumho Asiana Group Wing symbol.

No public source reviewed itemizes JaeYoun Park’s individual responsibilities on the Asiana project. The featured case is therefore labeled as studio/team work rather than personal ownership.
